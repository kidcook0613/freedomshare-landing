What Many Owners Learn Too Late

These are common pain points we hear from owners before they seek professional guidance.

Rising Fees Rarely Slow Down

Annual maintenance charges often climb year after year, leaving owners with a bill that gets heavier over time and very few built-in exits.

Typical owners report paying about $1,800 to $3,200 per year in maintenance costs.

Resale Value Is Usually Far Below What You Paid

Many buyers are told timeshares hold value, but resale markets often show steep drops and limited demand after purchase.

In many markets, only a small share of contracts are ever resold successfully.

Long-Term Contract Language Can Affect Heirs

Clauses such as "in perpetuity" may create obligations that continue beyond the original owner, depending on contract terms and estate handling.

Timeshare-related obligations appear frequently in estate and probate disputes.

Resort Retention Teams Focus on Keeping Contracts Active

When owners ask to cancel directly, they are commonly routed to retention departments designed to preserve the account whenever possible.

Direct cancellation requests are often declined without an external strategy.
